# Firmcraft — Positioning Brief & Site Architecture

**Status:** Draft for Doyle's review. Direction document — copy comes after sign-off.
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Scope:** Entire firmcraft.ai site (6 pages)
**Author:** Cowork session, from Doyle's repositioning direction

---

## 1. What changed

The site currently sells **packages**. The new positioning sells **judgment**.

That's the whole shift, and it breaks more of the existing site than a copy pass can fix. Today firmcraft.ai is structured like a productized agency: three phases, four named builds, three retainer tiers, a pricing page with a `Most popular` badge. That structure tells a visitor "here is the shelf, pick something." An advisory practice doesn't have a shelf.

### The new thesis

> Firmcraft is an advisory practice. Doyle Dettro is a CPA and working ERP consultant who helps organizations take control of their AI and technology stack **from the top** — then implements it, and stays on to support it as AI keeps moving.
>
> Firmcraft does not sell software. It advises on software, supports it, replaces it, or tells you to keep what you already have. That independence is the product.

Three things follow from this and should govern every page:

1. **Advisor first, implementer second.** The ability to build is credibility, not the offer. Plenty of shops can build. Very few can tell a CFO what *not* to build.
2. **Independence is the differentiator.** No reseller margin, no partner quota, no vendor relationship that survives contact with the client's interests. This must be said plainly, because right now the site accidentally argues the opposite.
3. **The relationship is ongoing, because AI won't hold still.** An implementation is a snapshot. The managed support layer is what keeps a company current as the landscape changes underneath them.

## 2. The customer's actual problem

Worth stating on the site in roughly these terms, because it's the thing the buyer recognizes and nobody else is naming:

AI arrived in the company **bottom-up**. Individual people bought individual tools. There are a dozen subscriptions nobody's counted, nothing is connected to the ERP, no one owns the whole picture, and the landscape changes faster than anyone on staff has time to track.

Meanwhile the buyer has two bad options:
- The **large consultancies** won't take a $10M–$500M company seriously.
- The **AI agencies** that will take the work can't read a chart of accounts.

The existing homepage hook already nails this — *"Big consulting firms won't touch you. AI agencies can't read your chart of accounts. You need both."* **Keep it.** It's the best sentence on the site and it survives the repositioning intact.

## 3. What Firmcraft does — service lines, not packages

**Doyle, Sep 2026: "There are no packages. That packages is AI slop from the SaaS software era. Firmcraft should be a serious advisory firm offering managed AI services and infrastructure."**

This retires *Assess → Build → Operate* (funnel language implying a purchase path) **and** the four named builds, the three retainer tiers, and the three Operator plans. Professional services firms have **service lines**, not SKUs. A law firm does not sell packages; neither does an audit practice.

Firmcraft's own description of itself, in Doyle's words, is the structure:

> a serious advisory firm offering **managed AI services and infrastructure**

### The three service lines

| Line | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Advisory** | Taking stock of what is running, what it costs, and what it actually does. Deciding what to keep, replace, build, or leave alone. Owning the AI and technology roadmap at the executive level. |
| **Managed AI services** | Running the setup on an ongoing basis. Monitoring, evaluation, tuning, retiring what has aged out, and bringing in what is genuinely new — so the client's AI capability keeps pace as the field moves. |
| **Infrastructure** | The technical foundation, with sovereignty as the default posture. Where client data lives, what runs inside the client's own environment, and what is acceptable to send elsewhere. |

**Implementation is not a fourth line.** It is the connective work that runs through all three, and describing it as a separate purchasable thing reintroduces exactly the product framing being removed.

### Hard constraints on how these are described

- No numbering or counting of offerings — no "four named builds," no "three tiers," no "Build · 01."
- No SKU names presented as buyable units (`Firmcraft Foundation`, `Firmcraft Finance`, `Essential / Standard / Comprehensive`, `Solo / Team / Pro`).
- No durations, no fee structures, no delivery-commitment badges.
- No `Most popular` / `Anchor` / `Most-asked` labels of any kind.
- Plain headings, complete sentences, described as **capability areas** a client engages with — not units they select from a list.

## 4. Register — the thing to fix most urgently

Doyle's note: *"take that car salesman language off the site; we are advisory, implementation, support backed by extensive experience and knowledge."*

### Kill these

| On the site now | Why it has to go |
|---|---|
| `Most popular` badge on a pricing card | Retail. Nothing else needs saying. |
| "running by Friday" (used repeatedly) | Urgency theater. Advisors don't promise speed as the headline. |
| "It's like hiring someone for the office — except it's $399 a month" | Price-anchored testimonial. Reads like an infomercial. |
| "pricing shifts from a sticker to a scoped engagement" | The word "sticker" is a car-lot word. |
| `$399/mo` inside `<title>` tags | SEO-driven price bait. |
| "Discovery call · 20 min · **free**" | The *offer* is fine. The **badge treatment** is not. |
| "Four named builds," "three retainer tiers" | Shelf language. |
| `Book a call →` repeated with arrow urgency | Too many CTAs, too insistent. |

### Replace with

Measured, specific, evidence-led. The proof is the credential and the method, not urgency. A visitor should come away thinking *"this person has actually done this"* — not *"I should act now."*

Concretely:
- State the first conversation costs nothing **in a sentence**, not a badge. Something closer to *"The first conversation doesn't cost anything — if it's not a fit, I'll say so."* No timer, no "FREE," no starburst.
- Let specificity do the persuading. "Half our assessments end with us telling the buyer they don't need AI — they need three Power Automate flows and a clean COA" is worth more than any CTA. Keep lines like that.
- One primary CTA per page. Suggested: **"Start a conversation."**

## 4b. The house voice — the second register problem

Separate from the promotional chrome above, and arguably worse. Doyle's call on the "Not a fit" line: **"that is AI slop language."** He's right, and it isn't one sentence — it's a style that runs the length of the site.

The underlying move: a punchy construction that lands on a "clever" specific detail meant to signal insider knowledge. It's a wink, not information. Catalogue of where it shows up:

| Pattern | Examples on the site now |
|---|---|
| **The dismissive specific** (the wink) | *"anyone whose answer to 'where does your AR data live?' is 'a Notion page'"* · *"a paid ChatGPT seat and a hope"* |
| **Sensory-metaphor flourish** | *"doesn't know what a chart of accounts smells like when it's been mishandled for four years"* |
| **Not-X-but-Y reversal** (heavily overused) | *"a roadmap you can actually fund — not a slide deck"* · *"Not a deck — a working artifact"* · *"The differentiator isn't the technology. It's the path through the technology"* · *"These aren't values for the careers page"* |
| **Self-congratulatory aside** | *"That answer alone usually pays for the Assessment."* · *"We don't sell follow-on work from this; we earn it."* · *"That's not a slogan — it's the operating constraint."* |
| **Repeated sentence frame** | *"Where the obsession with reconciled numbers got installed."* / *"Where the empathy got installed."* — twice in one timeline |
| **[Number] X. [Number] Y. headline** (≥5 uses) | *"Three phases. One operator."* · *"Four pivots. One throughline."* · *"Two tracks. One firm."* · *"Four things most AI shops can't say."* · *"Five phases. Inherited from ERP."* |

**Why this is load-bearing, not cosmetic:** the wink register contradicts the advisory positioning directly. An advisor's authority comes from being plain. Copy working this hard to seem sharp reads as someone auditioning rather than someone who has done the work. It's the same performance as the pricing badges — aimed at sounding smart instead of sounding urgent, but performance either way.

There's a business cost too. Dunking on Notion users is a strange move for someone selling judgment, and some of those people grow into customers or know someone who is.

### The rule for the copy pass

Write plainly. State what happens, what it costs the client in time and attention, what they get, and what you'd tell them not to do. Let the ICP data and the methodology carry the credibility — they already do.

**Specifically:**
- No jokes at a prospect's expense. If a segment isn't a fit, say so flat: *"Firmcraft works with companies already running an ERP. If your financial data lives in spreadsheets, this probably isn't the right call yet."*
- Retire the **[Number] X. [Number] Y.** headline entirely. Use a plain noun phrase.
- Cap the **not-X-but-Y** construction at roughly one per page. It's fine occasionally; right now it's the default sentence shape.
- No asides telling the reader how to feel about what they just read. Drop the line, keep the fact.
- The **qualification job belongs to the ICP block** — revenue, headcount, ERP, org gravity. That's data, and it qualifies harder than the joke did.

> ⚠️ Correction to §4 as first drafted: an earlier version of this brief said to *keep* lines like *"Half our assessments end with us telling the buyer they don't need an AI — they need three Power Automate flows and a clean COA."* That was wrong and is superseded by this section. The underlying **point** is excellent and must survive — Firmcraft regularly tells buyers not to build. Keep the substance, drop the delivery.

## 4c. The target register — professional, not stylish

**Doyle's instruction (Sep 2026): "I really just want to be professional and I don't like the youngster, stylish language at all."**

This supersedes §4 and §4b where they conflict, and it goes further than either. §4 removed promotional chrome. §4b removed the wink. **§4c removes the styling itself.** The target is the register of a well-run professional services firm — an audit practice, a law firm, a regional advisory shop. Authority comes from clarity and substance, not from craft.

### Rules

1. **Complete sentences.** No fragments used for emphasis. This eliminates most of the current headline style.
2. **Headings describe, they don't perform.** "What distinguishes Firmcraft," not "Four things most AI shops can't say."
3. **No rhetorical constructions** — no not-X-but-Y reversals, no triads building to a punch, no rhetorical questions, no punchlines.
4. **No em-dash-driven rhythm.** Use ordinary punctuation. An em dash where a comma or period works is a stylistic tic.
5. **Plain vocabulary.** Say "assessment," not "diagnostic." Say "roadmap," not "board-ready plan you can actually fund."
6. **No second-person swagger.** Address the reader plainly or write in the third person about the firm.

### Before / after, from the current site

| Current | Professional |
|---|---|
| "Big consulting firms won't touch you. AI agencies can't read your chart of accounts. You need both." | "Firmcraft advises mid-market companies on AI and technology strategy. We bring financial and ERP experience together with hands-on implementation — a combination large consultancies rarely offer at this size, and AI specialists rarely have at all." |
| "Four things most AI shops can't say." | "What distinguishes Firmcraft" |
| "A fixed-fee diagnostic that ends with a roadmap you can actually fund — not a slide deck." | "The assessment produces a prioritized twelve-month roadmap and a total cost of ownership model suitable for budget approval." |
| "ii. Sovereign by default. The default is Hermes, in your walls." | "Data sovereignty. Client data remains within the client's own infrastructure by default. Where a hosted model is the appropriate tool, we use one only when the data-handling terms are acceptable to the client." |
| "Three phases. One operator." | "How engagements are structured" |
| "The firm we wished existed when we were inside the controller's office." | "About Firmcraft" |

> ⚠️ This retires the current homepage hook. Earlier drafts of this brief said to keep *"Big consulting firms won't touch you…"* verbatim. That is superseded — it is advertising copy, not professional writing. **The underlying point survives and is still the core of the positioning:** Firmcraft occupies the gap between consultancies that won't serve this segment and AI specialists who lack financial-systems depth. State it in plain prose.

### Page furniture — same register problem

The styling affectations carry the same young/design-forward tone as the copy. If the copy is rewritten and the layout is not, the result is inconsistent. In scope for the same pass:

- Section labels: `01 · The practice`, `02 · Why Firmcraft`, `Phase 01 · Assess →`
- Roman-numeral principle lists (`i.` `ii.` `iii.` `iv.`)
- Decorative middot separators
- The founder pull-quote treatment on About
- Status-badge chrome: `operator.run · live`, `● Managed AI Operations`

A professional services firm numbers sections plainly or not at all.

## 4d. Firm voice and technology-agnosticism

**Doyle, Sep 2026: "Let's be less personal and also avoid naming specific technologies (like Business Central; use ERP instead); we'll be working across a wide variety of industries and technologies and I don't want to be boxed into anything at this stage."**

Two further constraints, both strategic rather than stylistic. Read together with §4c.

### Firm voice, not personal voice

The site is currently written in first-person-singular around Doyle by name. Move to firm voice.

| Current | Firm voice |
|---|---|
| "Doyle is a CPA and a working Microsoft Business Central consultant." | "Firmcraft's principal is a CPA who works in mid-market ERP implementation." |
| "Talk to Doyle →" | "Contact Firmcraft" |
| "We sit across from your controller, debate revenue-recognition treatment, then go build the LLM eval harness. Same person, same day." | "Engagements are led by someone who can work through a revenue recognition question with a controller and then build and test the implementation. That combination is the basis of the practice." |
| Founder pull-quote, "— Doyle Dettro · Founder" | Remove entirely |

**Keep in firm voice, do not lose:** the no-handoff commitment. "The person who scopes the work leads the implementation and remains through ongoing support. Engagements are not passed to a delivery team." It is a genuine differentiator and survives depersonalization intact.

**Open for Doyle:** the About page's four-phase dated timeline (Big-4 audit → industry controller → ERP consulting → builder) is a résumé, and the most personal element on the site. It is also the strongest credibility artifact. Recommendation is to compress to two or three sentences of firm background and drop the dated-phase treatment — but this is a real loss and Doyle may want it kept.

### No specific technology names

Product names come out. Categories stay. Rationale: Firmcraft will work across many industries and technologies, and naming products boxes the practice in prematurely. This also **resolves the reseller-menu contradiction in §6** — the vendor list was undercutting "we don't sell software."

| Current | Revised |
|---|---|
| "Microsoft Business Central implementations across field service, manufacturing, and operations" | "ERP implementations in manufacturing, field service, and distribution environments" |
| "Microsoft BC · NetSuite · Acumatica" | "Mid-market ERP platforms" |
| "ERP in place: BC · NetSuite · Acumatica · Sage" | "An ERP system in place, or actively replatforming" |
| "Finance — Vic.ai / Stampli + AP/AR workflows" | "Finance operations — accounts payable and receivable workflow" |
| "Operations — n8n agents + asset/maintenance" | "Operations — workflow automation across asset and maintenance processes" |
| "seven years inside Microsoft Business Central engagements at one of the larger Dynamics partners" | "several years of mid-market ERP implementation work" |

**Cost to manage:** product names were carrying credibility. A controller reads specificity as proof the work has actually been done. **Substitute industry and functional specificity for product specificity** — name the industries, the departments, and the processes instead of the software. That buys back most of the credibility.

**Consequence:** with personal detail and product names both removed, the **methodology page carries materially more weight.** It is impersonal, concrete, and product-neutral already, which makes it the strongest remaining proof. Treat it as the credibility centrepiece rather than a supporting page.

### Hermes — now a contradiction to resolve

The site currently states that every build engagement is "anchored on Hermes — our sovereign LLM foundation," and calls it "the substrate every other engagement extends."

Under vendor-neutrality plus technology-agnosticism, *"we always install our own platform"* is itself a lock-in claim — precisely what the positioning argues against.

**Reframe:** Hermes is what Firmcraft deploys **when a self-hosted, client-owned setup is the right answer** — not a mandatory substrate beneath every engagement. This keeps the sovereignty capability and the Apache-2.0 / client-owned proof point (still the strongest independence argument on the site) without implying a required product.

## 5. Pricing and packaging — both removed entirely

Per direction: **no pricing anywhere on the site.** This is more invasive than it sounds. Pricing is currently load-bearing:

- `/pricing` is a full page and appears **twice** in the header nav
- Homepage carries "$399–$1,499/mo" and "See plans"
- Services is built around "fixed-fee" as a structural promise
- Page `<title>` tags contain dollar figures
- The Operator section is entirely tier-driven

**All of it goes**, including from metadata. `/pricing` should 301 to the Advisory page rather than 404.

What replaces the *function* pricing was serving (qualification): the **Who it's for** block already on the homepage. Revenue band, headcount, ERP in place, finance- or ops-led, plus the "Not a fit" list. That block does the qualifying job honestly and it should be kept and possibly strengthened.

## 6. Vendor neutrality — currently argued backwards

`/services` names **Vic.ai, Stampli, n8n, Retell, Vapi** inside the build packages. Read cold, that's a reseller menu — which directly contradicts "we don't sell software."

Fix:
- Vendor names appear **only** as examples of things already integrated, in past tense, never as a menu of what's on offer.
- State the principle explicitly somewhere prominent. Draft: *"Firmcraft doesn't resell software and doesn't take vendor margin. When the answer is a tool you already own, that's the answer."*
- The existing operating principle *"Process before tool"* on `/about` is the right idea — promote it rather than bury it at position three.

## 7. Sovereignty & Hermes

Sovereignty is the posture, not a feature. Framing that already works and should be kept:

- Hermes is **Apache-2.0**, runs in the client's VPC or on-prem, *"we maintain it, you own it. If we disappear tomorrow, your operator keeps running."*

That line is the single best proof of independence on the site. It says the client isn't captured — which is exactly what an advisor with no software to sell should be able to say. Keep it and give it more room.

One adjustment: today Hermes is described as "the substrate every other engagement extends," which makes it sound like the product being sold. Reframe as **how sovereignty is delivered**, not what's being bought.


Four items, one CTA. Down from six items and a duplicated Pricing link.

### Page by page

#### Home
The whole argument in one scroll, no pricing, no Operator tiles.

2. The problem — AI arrived bottom-up, nobody owns it, nothing connects, it changes every 90 days
3. The three verbs — Advise / Implement / Support
4. Why this advisor — CPA + ERP, independence, sovereign by default, one person across the whole engagement

The three verbs in depth. Sovereignty section lives here with a link to the deep-dive. Vendor-neutrality principle stated plainly. **No durations, no fee structures, no package names.**

#### How We Work *(was `/methodology` — keep, elevate)*
**This is the strongest page on the site and needs the least work.** Five phases inherited from ERP — Discovery → Fit-gap → Configure → Train & eval → Hypercare. - What the operator actually does, who it suits, what it connects to
- **No tiers, no prices, no "Most popular"**
- Decide whether this page is the canonical home or a doorway to `operator.run` (see open questions)

#### About
Closest to right already. Keep: the thesis, the four pivots timeline, the Hermes section, and three of four operating principles.

- **Rewrite** principle iii, *"Fixed-fee where it matters"* — it's a pricing statement and pricing is off the site. Replace with something about scope discipline or independence.
- Keep *"Process before tool," "Sovereign by default,"* and *"One engineer, one engagement."* All three are advisor principles and all three are good.

#### Sovereignty *(promoted to its own page)*
What stays in your walls, what doesn't, how that call gets made, and what gets written into the contract rather than the marketing page. Linked from Advisory and the footer. This is a **proof page**, not a browse page — it exists so a CFO or IT lead can be sent a link.

## 10. Decisions — resolved 2026-09-19

1. **`operator.run` — RESOLVED, and it was a false premise.** `operator.run` has **no DNS record and does not resolve.** There is no separate Operator property. The `operator.run · live` badge on the homepage points at a domain that does not exist and must come out (see §9). **The `/for-small-business` section on firmcraft.ai is the Operator's only home.**

2. **Sovereignty — RESOLVED.** Footer link + standalone deep-dive page. Not primary nav. Top nav stays at four items.

3. **SkillCalibrate — RESOLVED: yes, it can appear on firmcraft.ai.** Prospects already cross between the two — the Pilson lead arrived through the SkillCalibrate contact form and was answered as Firmcraft. The endorsed-brand construction is *"SkillCalibrate by Firmcraft."* Treat Firmcraft as the parent practice and SkillCalibrate as the named capability underneath it. Placement to be worked out in the copy pass; likely a short block on About plus a footer link, not a nav slot.

4. **Nav label — RESOLVED: use "Advisory."** Rationale below, since this one is load-bearing.

### Why "Advisory" over "Services"

- **It's the buyer's native word.** The ICP is finance- and ops-led — CFO, controller, Director of FP&A. In accounting-firm vocabulary the three pillars are Audit, Tax, and **Advisory**. To that reader, "Advisory" is not jargon; it's the established word for *the people you call when you need judgment rather than a deliverable*. Doyle being a CPA makes it doubly native.
- **"Services" puts Firmcraft in the wrong comparison set.** Every AI agency has a Services page. If the entire repositioning is "I am not an agency with a shelf," leading the nav with the agency word undercuts it before anyone reads a sentence.
- **The nav is the first structural signal.** *Advisory · How We Work · For Small Business · About* already reads like a professional practice. Swap in "Services" and the same four items read like a vendor.

**The counter-argument, acknowledged:** search volume favors "AI consulting services." That's real — but nav labels aren't where that's won. Target the search terms in `<title>`, H1, and body copy; let the nav carry the positioning.
